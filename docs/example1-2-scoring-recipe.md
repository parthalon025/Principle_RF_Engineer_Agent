## Example 1 — Mie-resonance dielectric cube, "magnetic mirror"

**Recap of the criterion (from #107, not re-derived):** reflection phase ∠Γ ≈ 0° over the band; the patent's own retrieved effective permeability rises from µ_eff≈5 to ≈20 at resonance; "the conventional AMC bandwidth is the span where ∠Γ lies within ±90° of 0°."

### 1. Primary objective

**Score on the worst-case in-band phase deviation from 0°, minimax — not a mean, not the resonance-peak value.** Formally: minimize `max_{f∈Band} |∠Γ(f)|`, or equivalently (the same criterion read as a bandwidth rather than a scalar) report the span over which `|∠Γ(f)| ≤ Φ/2` holds *everywhere*, for a phase-window Φ.

**Why minimax, and why this isn't invented for this task:** it is the direct transfer of ADR-0041 point 1's already-settled reasoning (worst-in-band absorptivity, not mean, "a single lucky deep null can drag an average down and make a design that fails badly everywhere else look good on paper") from a power criterion to a phase criterion — same failure mode, same fix: a design that hits ∠Γ=0° at one frequency but drifts to ±150° a little off it would look fine on a mean-phase-error metric while being a poor magnetic mirror across most of its stated band.

It is also **not just an analogy — it is how the field itself defines AMC bandwidth**, and this is verified first-hand rather than through a secondary summary. Gustafsson & Sjöberg's own derivation *defines* the bandwidth `B` as the region where the admittance stays under a threshold **everywhere**, not on average:

> "We are interested in regions with Γ ≈ 1 or equivalently |Z| ≥ ∆⁻¹ where 0 < ∆ < 1 is a number used to quantify the high-impedance surface. It is also convenient to consider the normalized admittance Y = 1/Z that has |Y| ≤ ∆ for high impedance surfaces."
> — Gustafsson & Sjöberg (2010), §4, **p.7** (verbatim, extracted directly from the primary PDF)

That `|Y(λ)| ≤ ∆` **for all** `λ` in the band is a worst-case/minimax membership test by construction — it is literally the same shape ADR-0041 point 4 already found in the absorber-angle literature ("every conformal/curved-absorber paper checked... reports discrete-angle threshold-retention statements, even where a full continuous angle-sweep curve is already sitting in the paper's own plotted data"): the field reports a *retained* band, not an *averaged* figure of merit.

**Retrieved µ_eff is diagnostic, not scored.** Per ADR-0022 ("state the mechanism and the prediction before evaluating"), µ_eff(f) is the *mechanism check* that explains why ∠Γ behaves as it does — it is not an independent thing to maximize. Chasing a higher peak µ_eff (5→20 is the patent's own claimed range) is not itself a design goal; a candidate that hits ∠Γ≈0° with µ_eff=6 is exactly as good a magnetic mirror as one that needs µ_eff=20 to get there. This mirrors ADR-0041's own separation of the scored objective (points 1–4) from the reproduction-comparison mechanism (point 5) — two different things that happen to use overlapping data.

### 2. Default when the customer is silent

**±90° (Φ=π) is a reversible default, never a hardcoded threshold.** This is a real, named literature convention — "the reflection phase of an AMC surface varies continuously from +180° to −180°... a useful bandwidth may be obtained" bracketed at ±90° — traced by secondary sources to Sievenpiper et al. (1999), *"High-Impedance Electromagnetic Surfaces with a Forbidden Frequency Band,"* IEEE Trans. Microwave Theory Tech. 47(11), 2059–2074, and formalized by Yang & Rahmat-Samii (2003), *"Reflection Phase Characterizations of the EBG Ground Plane for Low Profile Wire Antenna Applications,"* IEEE Trans. Antennas Propag. 51(10), 2691–2703. **`LITERATURE-SUPPORTED`, cited via consistent secondary summary — neither primary paper's full text was independently fetched in this pass**, the same confidence tier this project already applies to Rozanov 2000 and Weile 1996.

**This is exactly the shape of number `docs/requirement-derived-thresholds.md` already warns about.** That document's whole reason for existing is a phase-budget convention (±22.5°, a 3-bit-quantization half-step) that got imported as a *gate* rather than a *default* and silently made a rule six times stricter than intended. ±90° is the same kind of number in the same units (degrees of phase) doing the same kind of work — it must be recorded as a filled-in default when the customer states no phase-window requirement, and overridden without argument the moment they do (e.g., a lower-profile-antenna application may need a tighter window than ±90°; a customer indifferent to antenna VSWR degradation off-resonance may accept a looser one). Hardcoding it would repeat, in a new family, the exact mistake `requirement-derived-thresholds.md` was written to stop.

**No default is needed for the retrieved-µ_eff value itself** — it isn't a threshold at all, so there's nothing to fill.

### 3. Physical bound

**Gustafsson & Sjöberg applies — this is the same family already used for Examples 4/5/7, and this can be confirmed directly rather than by inference.** Two facts, both read first-hand from the primary text rather than restated:

> "The sum rule is valid for periodic structures composed by **arbitrary dielectric and magnetic materials** above a perfect conductor."
> — Gustafsson & Sjöberg (2010), §1, **p.1** (verbatim)

and their own general-form bound is stated as **realization-independent**:

> "`Bλ₀/d ≤ 4πµ_s^max·max_{λ∈B}|Y(λ)|` × {1 lossy case; 1/2 lossless case} (4.10). This version of the bound is independent of the specific realization of the high impedance surface."
> — Gustafsson & Sjöberg (2010), §4, Eq. (4.10) and surrounding text, p.9 (verbatim)

That is not a metal-patch-specific result being stretched to cover Example 1 — the paper's own scope statement already includes "arbitrary dielectric" structures, and its §6.1 worked numerical example is a **bare dielectric slab** over a ground plane (εr=1 and εr=4 cases), reaching `Bλ₀/d≈2.6` at `B/B_bound≈82%` — a non-patterned, all-dielectric AMC, structurally the closest thing in their own paper to Example 1's function (though not identical to Example 1's discrete Mie-resonant-cube geometry).

**Why Rozanov, not this bound, is the wrong fit here — worth stating explicitly since both are in the #129/ADR-0047 table.** A magnetic mirror wants `|Γ|→1` (near-total, 0°-phase reflection); Rozanov's sum rule bounds how much can be *absorbed* per unit thickness (`|Γ|→0` is the interesting direction there). These are opposite regimes of the same reflection coefficient — Example 1 belongs with the reflection-*phase* family (G&S), not the absorption family (Rozanov), for the identical reason ADR-0047 already puts Examples 4/5/7 there and Example 3 with Rozanov.

**A genuinely new derived point, flagged as such — not stated by any source found, but built from two verified first-hand.** G&S's `µ_s^max` is explicitly the **static (DC, ω→0)** permeability (their Eq. 3.5's magnetostatic-field derivation), the same requirement Rozanov's `µ_s` carries and that `docs/absorber-thickness-bandwidth-bound.md` §4 already documents for absorbers ("the bound uses the static (DC) permeability"). Mie-resonance-induced effective permeability is a resonant (Lorentzian-type) dispersion that is well-established in the metamaterial literature to return to ≈1 away from the resonance — a purely dielectric, non-ferrimagnetic material has no static magnetic response at all. **Putting these two facts together: the patent's own headline number (µ_eff≈20 at resonance) does not loosen this bound the way it would for a true ferrite** (where a high *static* µ_s buys real bandwidth budget, per `docs/absorber-thickness-bandwidth-bound.md` §4's Snoek's-law table for Rozanov) — because the resonant boost vanishes exactly at the DC limit the bound cares about. If this holds, Example 1's own dielectric cube is bound by the **non-magnetic case**, `Bλ₀/d ≤ π` (Eq. 4.11), the same ceiling as an ordinary non-resonant dielectric AMC, not something loosened by its own claimed 20× resonant permeability. **This could not be independently verified against the patent's own FIG. 5F low-frequency asymptote** (not re-rendered in this pass) — see §4 below.

Using this project's own established reference point (9.5 GHz, λ₀=31.5571 mm, the 0.87/2.00 mm envelope already tabulated for Rozanov and for the ±45° steering case) purely for **illustrative, comparative** arithmetic — **not** a claim about Example 1's own stated band, which #107 does not record:

| Thickness | Φ=π (±90°, AMC/magnetic-mirror, lossless) | Φ=π/2 (±45°, steering, already tabulated) |
|---|---|---|
| 0.87 mm | `2π×0.87/31.5571` = **17.4%** | 7.2% |
| 2.00 mm | `2π×2.00/31.5571` = **39.8%** | 16.5% |

`CALCULATED` from Eq. (5.1)'s own `2π, Φ=π` value. The ±90° AMC criterion is ≈2.4× more permissive than the ±45° steering criterion at the same thickness (`2π/2.6=2.415`, consistent) — a magnetic-mirror tolerance is a much looser phase ask than a precise steering phase, so it should buy proportionately more bandwidth, and it does. **One more caveat that must travel with this table:** Eq. (5.1)'s closed forms (`2.6`, `2π`, …) are explicitly derived only for the **lossless** case (`Γ=e^{-jφ}`, unit amplitude); Example 1's own SrTiO₃ insert is not lossless (ε₁=250−1.25j, tanδ≈0.005), so the applicable bound is the more general, and more permissive, `4π·µ_s^max·max|Y|` lossy branch of Eq. (4.10) — not evaluated numerically here because it needs a stated reflectivity target (`max|Y(λ)|`) that Example 1's own criterion, as recorded in #107, doesn't state.

**Recommendation for the registry (not itself an ADR):** extend ADR-0047's per-family table's "beam steering by reflection phase / AMC-checkerboard backscatter" row to explicitly name Example 1 as a member, on the same G&S citation, with the static-vs-resonant-µs caveat above carried as a footnote specific to Mie-resonance realizations of that family.

### 4. What remains open

- **The static-µs argument above is its own synthesis, not a stated literature conclusion for this exact case.** No paper was found stating "a Mie-resonance dielectric magnetic mirror's Gustafsson-Sjöberg bandwidth is governed by its non-magnetic case despite a high resonant µ_eff." It follows from combining two independently verified facts (G&S's own static-permeability requirement; the general, textbook Lorentzian-dispersion behavior of resonance-induced effective permeability), but the missing measurement that would actually settle it is: **read FIG. 5F's low-frequency asymptote** from the patent's own drawing. If µ_eff genuinely returns to ≈1 well below resonance, the argument holds; if the drawing shows an elevated low-frequency plateau (a second, lower-order resonance or a static contribution not accounted for), it doesn't. This was not checked in this pass.
- **Whether the free-standing retrieved µ_eff/ε_eff (FIG. 5D–F, no ground plane, per #107) transfers unchanged to a ground-backed deployment is a real, unresolved modeling question**, not just a formality. G&S's bound needs the structure to actually sit above a PEC; the patent's own simulated/measured Example 1 doesn't (two-port, transmission exists) — the identical situation ADR-0047 already flagged for Rozanov and Example 3 ("the bound does not govern Example 3, by this document's own assumption... Example 3 has no ground plane"). The parallel case *does* exist in the literature — Su, Zhao, Song, Zhao & Yin, *"Electrically tunable metasurface based on Mie-type dielectric resonators,"* Sci. Rep. 7, 43026 (2017), doi:10.1038/srep43026 (PMC5318854), demonstrates Mie-resonant ferroelectric disks arrayed directly on a metal film achieving controllable reflection phase — so the mechanism family is real and buildable in principle. But whether the *specific* retrieved µ_eff curve from a free-standing measurement carries over quantitatively once a conductor sits in the resonator's near field (image-dipole coupling can shift or damp a Mie resonance at close range) is not addressed by that paper or by anything else found, and is not computed here.
- **Confidence tier on the ±90° convention itself is secondary-source-level**, consistent with how this project already treats Rozanov 2000 and Weile 1996 — worth a primary-text check before this becomes a hardcoded default anywhere in code.

## Example 2 — Dual-cube, impedance match to free space

**Recap of the criterion (from #107, not re-derived):** ε_eff(f) ≈ µ_eff(f) at the same frequency, i.e. η_eff = η₀ (impedance matched to free space); "resonances overlapping; no refraction." Two-port, no ground plane, per #107 — and structurally *can't* have one, since the function (letting a wave through unimpeded) is the opposite of a mirror.

### 1. Primary objective

**Minimize the worst-case in-band residual reflection, minimax, on the same reasoning as Example 1 and ADR-0041 point 1:** `max_{f∈Band} |Γ_eff(f)|`, where `Γ_eff(f) = (η_eff(f)−η₀)/(η_eff(f)+η₀)` and `η_eff=√(µ_eff/ε_eff)·η₀` — equivalently, minimize the worst-case normalized mismatch between the two retrieved curves, since the target condition is literally `ε_eff(f)=µ_eff(f)` everywhere in-band. A design that matches perfectly at one frequency and drifts badly elsewhere is a poor "matched over the band" candidate even if its average mismatch looks good — the identical argument Yiğit & Duysak (2021, already cited in ADR-0041) make against averaging absorptivity applies here without modification.

**Why this specific quantity, not a bare `|ε_eff−µ_eff|` difference:** using the voltage reflection coefficient `Γ_eff` ties the scored objective directly to the same quantity any physical_bound for this family would have to be phrased in terms of (see §3) — so a later "% of the limit" report (per ADR-0047's own rule, "the number the loop reports is the integral of the candidate's own simulated response curve against the bound's inequality") is well-defined without a unit-conversion step in between.

**"No refraction" is a second condition #107 records but doesn't fully unpack, worth flagging rather than quietly folding in:** impedance match (`η_eff=η₀`) only requires `ε_eff=µ_eff`, at *any* common value — it does not by itself require `n_eff=√(ε_effµ_eff)=1`. A slab with `ε_eff=µ_eff=4`, say, is perfectly impedance-matched (no reflection at normal incidence) but still has a refractive index of 2, not 1 — light slows down and, at oblique incidence, genuinely bends entering it. Whether the patent's "no refraction" phrase means the stronger condition (`ε_eff=µ_eff=1`, true free-space-equivalence) or is loosely describing the impedance-match condition alone is not resolved anywhere in #107, and is not resolved here either — it changes what "passing" requires by a full extra constraint.

### 2. Default when the customer is silent

**No literature-verified numeric default specific to this function was found**, unlike the absorber case where `docs/absorber-scoring-conventions.md`/`absorber-scoring-decision-confirmation.md` did a documented literature search and landed on −10 dB/90% with named sources. The nearest thing is a structural coincidence, not a citation: standard antenna/radome engineering commonly treats −10 dB return loss (VSWR≈1.92) as a "good match" mark, which is arithmetically the *same number* as the absorber default (`|Γ|²=0.100` either way) — but this is not verified as an actually-used convention for an "impedance-matched-to-free-space metasurface" function anywhere in the metasurface/matching-layer literature searched in this pass (the Presutti & Monticone and Zheng/Hao/Li papers below both report their own worked numbers, e.g. reflectance "suppressed below 0.1" in one search summary of the latter, without establishing it as a field-wide default). **Honest answer: no verified literature default for this exact function; if one is needed before a real convention search is done, `|Γ|=0.316` (−10 dB) is a defensible engineering-analogy placeholder by arithmetic coincidence with the absorber default, not an adopted convention — a judgment call, not a citation.**

### 3. Physical bound — genuinely new, not in #129/ADR-0047's table for any family

**Neither Rozanov nor Gustafsson-Sjöberg fits, and the mismatch is structural, not incidental.** Both require a PEC backing in their own derivations (Rozanov: "a multilayer metal-backed slab"; G&S: "periodic structures... above a perfect conductor," and their causality argument literally uses a mirror-image construction through that conductor). Example 2's function is the opposite of a mirror — a wave is meant to pass through, not reflect — so there is no ground plane in either the patent's own simulated setup *or* in any physically sensible deployment of this function. This is a cleaner exclusion than Example 1's (where the ground-plane mismatch was only a difference between the retrieval setup and the intended use).

**What does fit is the same causality/passivity machinery, applied to a different boundary condition — a slab matched to free space on both faces rather than backed by a conductor.** This is the classical Bode-Fano broadband-matching theorem (R. Fano, *"Theoretical limitations on the broadband matching of arbitrary impedances,"* J. Franklin Inst. 249(1,2), 57–83 and 139–154, 1950 — the same foundational citation G&S's own paper lists as their ref [4]), specialized to a matched slab rather than a lumped load. A primary-source derivation of exactly this specialization was found and independently verified (source page rendered as an image, typeset equation read directly):

> "...the metalens is impedance matched with respect to the medium in which the incident wave propagates... a fundamental trade-off exists between the reflection reduction and the continuous bandwidth over which this reduction can be achieved. This fundamental limit on broadband impedance matching is known as the Bode-Fano limit... This bound depends uniquely on the linearity, passivity, time-invariance, and causality of the scattering system, and, most importantly, is independent of the employed anti-reflection coating, regardless of its complexity (the matching structure is only assumed to be lossless)."
>
> "`Δω/ωc ≤ [1/(L/λc·(ε−εb))]·[log(1/|Γ|)]⁻¹`, where `ε=n_avg²` and Γ is the in-band reflection coefficient. If the equal sign is used, Eq. (14) represents the optimal trade-off between bandwidth and reflection reduction."
> — Presutti, F. & Monticone, F., *"Focusing on bandwidth: achromatic metalens limits,"* Optica 7, 624–631 (2020), arXiv:2001.10899, §"Bandwidth limits on reflection suppression," verbatim, verified by rendering the primary PDF page as an image (not text-extraction alone, which garbled the fraction on a first pass)

Rearranged, `B·ln(1/|Γ|) ≤ λc/(L·(ε−εb))` where `B=Δω/ωc` — the **identical shape** as Rozanov's `|lnρ₀|·Δλ ≤ 2π²µ_sd`: a product of (fractional bandwidth) and (a log matching-quality term) bounded by (thickness × a material contrast term). The physics is the same family; the boundary condition is different — matched-to-free-space instead of PEC-backed. This is the honest answer to whether Examples 1/2 "need deriving fresh": **Example 1 doesn't (G&S already covers it, once ground-backed); Example 2 does, and the derivation exists in the open literature but had not been connected to this project's registry before now.**

**Note on the log base in the equation above (a follow-on caveat, not a defect in this document as written):** the source page's own typeset `log(1/|Γ|)` doesn't state its base explicitly. No numeric conclusion in this document currently depends on the answer (a worked numeric example is deliberately withheld below, for the same reason ADR-0047/#129 already learned not to force one), so this is a note for whoever eventually computes a number from Eq. (14), not a defect here.

**The direct-hit paper for Example 2's exact language — found, DOI-confirmed, but unread.** Zheng, W., Hao, T. & Li, X., *"Thickness-to-Bandwidth Limit for Impedance Matching Metasurfaces Design According to the Bode-Fano Theorem,"* IEEE Trans. Microwave Theory Tech. 73(11), 8451–8463 (2025), doi:10.1109/TMTT.2025.3592449 — confirmed to exist via CrossRef (title, authors, journal, volume/issue/pages, DOI all independently verified). Per search-engine synthesis (not independently read — IEEE-paywalled, the same stranding pattern this project already has for Rozanov 2000 itself): it "derive[s] a general form of the thickness-to-bandwidth limit according to the Bode–Fano theorem for the transmissive impedance-matching metasurfaces... by effectively manipulating key parameters, for example, relative permittivity and permeability" — i.e., explicitly Example 2's own ε/µ-manipulation mechanism, and explicitly transmissive (not PEC-backed) — the closest on-point source found, but `LITERATURE-SUPPORTED, exact formula unverified`, not `CONFIRMED`.

**A precedent one step closer to Example 2's specific physical setup**, also verified first-hand: Monticone, F. & Alù, A., *"Invisibility exposed: physical bounds on passive cloaking,"* Optica 3, 718–724 (2016), doi:10.1364/OPTICA.3.000718 — the source Presutti & Monticone cite (their ref [34]) for Eq. (14) itself, applying the same Bode-Fano machinery to a passive-cloaking/matching-shell problem rather than a mirror — its full text could not be fetched (JS-gated journal page) in this pass, so this relies on Presutti & Monticone's own citation of it rather than reading it directly.

No illustrative "% of bound" number is computed for Example 2 the way one was for Example 1, because doing so would need a stated `ε_avg`/`εb` contrast and reflection target that #107 doesn't record for this example, and forcing round numbers here risks repeating exactly the mistake #129 made before its correction (computing "where Example 3 sits" against a bound using an axis label mistaken for a requirement).

**Recommendation for the registry (not itself an ADR):** propose a new #129/ADR-0047 table row — "impedance-matched/transmissive surfaces" — citing Fano (1950) as the foundational theorem and Presutti & Monticone (2020)/Monticone & Alù (2016) as the verified slab-specific specialization, with Zheng, Hao & Li (2025) flagged as the most directly on-point but unverified source to chase down before this becomes load-bearing.

### 4. What remains open

- **The exact formula is verified for a metalens-approximation slab (uniform index, Fabry-Perot-favorable center frequency), not for Example 2's actual dual-cube geometry.** Presutti & Monticone's own caveat: "This is clearly a coarse approximation, but it allows us to get some general insight." Whether the two-cube-size unit cell's own retrieved ε_eff/µ_eff dispersion is well-approximated by a single `n_avg` the way their derivation assumes is unchecked.
- **The Zheng/Hao/Li (2025) paper is the single most promising unresolved lead in this whole write-up** — it appears, by title and abstract-level description alone, to be closer to Example 2's exact problem (ε/µ-manipulated transmissive matching metasurfaces, explicitly invoking AMC concepts) than the metalens paper is, but its content past the IEEE paywall could not be confirmed to match, extend, or differ from Eq. (14) above. This is the clearest "name the missing measurement" item in this document: someone with IEEE Xplore access should read it before this bound is coded up.
- **Whether `ε` in Eq. (14) should be the constituent insert's retrieved permittivity or the homogenized unit-cell's effective permittivity is unresolved here**, for the same reason Padilla et al. (already cited in `docs/absorber-thickness-bandwidth-bound.md` §3) flagged for Rozanov's `µ_s`: a metamaterial's bound-relevant material parameter is the *retrieved effective* one, not the constituent material's raw value. This has not been confirmed to transfer identically to Eq. (14)'s `ε=n_avg²`.
- **The "no refraction" ambiguity from §1 directly affects what this bound should be checked against** — if the true pass condition is `ε_eff=µ_eff=1` rather than merely `ε_eff=µ_eff`, the relevant `(ε−εb)` contrast in Eq. (14) would need to shrink toward zero as the design approaches the stricter target, which changes the bound's practical bite. Not resolved here.

## Cross-cutting notes (both examples)

**Reproduction/validation curve comparison, if ever needed:** per #107, unlike Example 3, both Examples 1 and 2 have the patent's *own* measured curves (FIG. 5D–F; FIG. 6D–E) — so there is no need to reach for a third-party paper the way Example 3 needed Landy 2008. **The comparison method should still be FSV, not RMS error, for the identical reason ADR-0041 point 5 already settled**: these are resonance-shaped curves (Mie resonance is a sharp feature by construction), exactly the class of data FSV (IEEE Std 1597.1/1597.2) was built to compare correctly where naive point-by-point RMS over-penalizes a small resonance-frequency shift. This is an application of that decision, not a re-derivation of it — the underlying curve shape is the same problem.

**A genuinely new wrinkle FSV's existing spec (issue #168) doesn't cover, worth naming plainly rather than silently assuming it generalizes:** Example 3 has one curve to compare (absorptivity). Examples 1 and 2 each have **multiple** curves that must be reconciled *jointly* — Example 1's |S|, ∠S, and µ_eff (three curves, FIG. 5D/E/F); Example 2's ε_eff and µ_eff, which must not only each track a reference but also **coincide with each other** at the same frequency (the actual pass condition). No literature on multi-curve joint FSV was found, and #168 as scoped (per ADR-0041's own text) only covers a single absorptivity curve. Whether FSV's ADM/FDM/GDM machinery composes cleanly across several jointly-constrained curves, or needs its own extension, is an open question this document surfaces but does not answer.

**Both examples are permanently excluded from this program's fabrication path (#108: no printing/lamination route produces a solid 3D ceramic block), and nothing above changes that.** Per ADR-0021, they remain scored candidates reported with their exclusion reason — the recipes above are what let the loop say *how close* a hypothetical bulk-ceramic build would come to its own family's physical bound, even though the answer to "can we build it" stays no.
