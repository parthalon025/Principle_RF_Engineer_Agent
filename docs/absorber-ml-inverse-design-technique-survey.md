# ML inverse-design technique for ABSORBER (Tier A pilot): which class, and does differentiability change the answer

**Date:** 2026-09-12
**Ticket:** [#559](https://github.com/parthalon025/Principle_RF_Engineer_Agent/issues/559), a child of the map issue [#556](https://github.com/parthalon025/Principle_RF_Engineer_Agent/issues/556) ("ML inverse design: from target response to candidate geometry").
**Question:** `ABSORBER` has a working closed-form `analysis_model` (`rf_tools/absorber.py`'s Costa/Luukkonen model) that can cheaply bulk-generate training pairs with no solver in the loop, and new ML dependencies are authorized. Which class of ML inverse-design technique is appropriate — a direct regression network, a conditional generative model, or an autodiff-based inversion of the closed-form model itself? Does the closed-form model's own differentiability change the answer?

---

## Bottom line up front

**Neither a regression network nor a generative network.** For `ABSORBER` specifically, the appropriate technique is **gradient-based inversion of the closed-form Costa/Luukkonen model itself, via automatic differentiation** — not a model *trained* on the closed form's outputs at all. Read plainly: don't teach a second, approximate model to imitate the exact one we already trust; instead, hand the exact one to an automatic-differentiation library so a gradient-descent search can run directly against it. The two "ML" options in the ticket's own framing (regression network, conditional generative model) both spend a new dataset, a training pipeline, and an approximation-error tax to reproduce a mapping that is already exact, already `CALCULATED`-provenance, and already cheap to evaluate (`rf_tools/absorber.py:absorptivity` is a handful of elementary complex-arithmetic operations — microseconds, not minutes). Reading `rf_tools/absorber.py` and `rf_tools/sheet_impedance.py` line by line finds no root-finding, no lookup table, and no branch on a continuous design variable that would block differentiation — the model is differentiable in principle exactly as published, and needs a one-for-one syntactic port (`math`/`cmath` → `jax.numpy`, mostly) rather than a re-derivation, to make that principle usable. This sharpens, rather than contradicts, ADR-0039 point 8: that decision's "ML-direct third tier" is a genuinely *learned* surrogate gated on historical design data that does not yet exist. Autodiff-based inversion of an already-existing closed form needs no such data and isn't waiting on that gate at all — arguably it isn't "ML" in the sense the ADR means, and #559's own title should be read with that caveat.

## 1. What this repo has already read, and where it points

**`docs/ai-metasurface-survey-against-the-seven-examples.md`** is a secondhand survey reading (`LITERATURE-SUPPORTED` *at one remove*, by its own caveat) but it puts two load-bearing facts on the record:

- Example 3 (absorber, `ABSORBER`'s own literature anchor) has a direct precedent for the **regression-surrogate** class: *"ACDL reaches 94% prediction accuracy on 7,000 samples for a 10-parameter absorber"* (§2). That is the exact shape of "a direct regression network trained on the closed-form model's bulk-generated outputs" the ticket asks about, just trained on a full-wave corpus instead of a closed-form one.
- §7, quoting the survey's own Challenges section, states the reason none of that changes with more data: inverse design *"faces significant challenges owing to the complex, non-unique relationship between the structure and electromagnetic response, where multiple geometries can yield similar responses,"* and the repo's own reading of that is blunt — **"this is not a data-volume problem and cannot be spent away — which is a reason to prefer forward search with a cheap surrogate... over inverse design, independent of cost."** That conclusion was reached for the general case; it applies with more force here, because `ABSORBER` already has a cheap, exact forward evaluator on hand (§3 below), so "forward search over a cheap surrogate" is not a fallback — it is a strictly better option than either learned class, at no data or training cost at all.

**ADR-0039** (`docs/adr/0039-two-tier-em-modelling-hybrid-fast-tier-meep-and-emerge.md`) is the decision record that put the Costa/Luukkonen closed form in place as `ABSORBER`'s fast tier (point 1: *"closed-form algebra... Costa et al. for the absorber stack"*), and separately reasons about a third, ML-direct tier:

> **"An ML-direct inverse-design surrogate is a legitimate third tier, alongside the fast and confirmation tiers... It is adopted once enough historical design data exists to train on, which is not yet the case."** Chen's 2023 UIC dissertation validates one such approach — an autoencoder-decoder trained on ~30,000 HFSS simulations — on three real absorber-radome cases, the nearest existing precedent.

Read carefully, this is a **learned-surrogate** tier, gated on **historical design-loop data**, and its only cited precedent (Chen 2023) trains on tens of thousands of full-wave solves. Two things follow for `ABSORBER`: first, the ADR never says the training corpus must come from a solver — nothing here forecloses training on closed-form-generated pairs, so the letter of the gate ("enough historical design data") is arguably satisfiable today by bulk-generating from `absorber_band_response`, at effectively zero marginal cost per sample. Second, and this is the finding this document adds: **that whole framing — dataset, training, a learned mapping to approximate — is the wrong shape for this family regardless of how cheap the dataset is to make**, because the thing being approximated is already fully known, closed-form, and fast enough to differentiate directly. Training a network to imitate a function you can already evaluate and now differentiate exactly is strictly worse than differentiating the function itself: same forward cost, an added approximation-error term, plus a training/validation pipeline `ABSORBER` does not need. ADR-0039 was written before this ticket asked whether the closed form itself is differentiable; §2 below answers that, and §4 draws the consequence.

## 2. Is `absorber_band_response`/`absorptivity` differentiable as published?

Reading `rf_tools/absorber.py` and the `rf_tools/sheet_impedance.py` functions it calls (`capacitive_grid_sheet_capacitance_f`, `grid_effective_permittivity`, `grid_gap_loss_tangent`, `path_resistance_from_squares`), the full call graph behind one absorption value is:

```
absorptivity
├── grounded_slab_impedance      cmath.sqrt, cmath.tan, complex arithmetic
└── patterned_sheet_impedance
    ├── path_resistance_from_squares          plain multiplication
    └── capacitive_grid_sheet_capacitance_f
        ├── grid_effective_permittivity        (eps_r + 1) / 2
        ├── math.sin, math.log
        └── thin_spacer_capacitance_correction_f   math.log1p, math.exp
```

**No root-finding, no lookup table, no iterative solve, and no branch on a continuous design variable.** Every operation is an elementary function (`sqrt`, `tan`, `sin`, `log`, `log1p`, `exp`) or ordinary complex arithmetic (`+`, `*`, `/`, `abs`), all of which have standard, well-behaved derivatives almost everywhere. The branches that do exist are all **structural**, not data-dependent in the sense that would break a gradient:

- `patterned_sheet_impedance`'s `if gap_m is None:` chooses between a patterned-sheet model and an unpatterned-sheet model — a discrete choice of *which physical structure* is being described, exactly like choosing which family to instantiate; it is not something a continuous optimizer would ever need to move through, because `gap_m` stays a real gap (or stays absent) for the whole search.
- `absorptivity`'s `z_in = (z_s * z_d) / denom if abs(denom) > 0 else complex(float("inf"), 0)` is a numerical-safety guard for the zero-measure case of an exactly-cancelling denominator; for any physically sensible stack this branch is never taken, and where it matters it can be replaced by a smooth guard (`jnp.where`) without changing any real answer.
- `absorber_band_response`'s clamp, `min(1.0, max(0.0, absorbed))`, and its worst/best selection, `min(curve, key=...)`/`max(curve, key=...)`, are both differentiable almost everywhere in the standard subgradient sense — this is exactly how `jnp.clip` and `jnp.min`/`jnp.max` behave, and the frequency sweep the current Python `for` loop performs is embarrassingly parallel, so it is also a natural fit for `jax.vmap` rather than a loop.
- **One branch that looks relevant but is not reached here**: `sheet_impedance.sheet_resistance_ohm_sq`'s `if abs(arg) > 30: coth = 1` guard (its skin-depth/thickness correction) is never called from `absorptivity` or `absorber_band_response` — both take `sheet_resistance_ohm_sq` as a caller-supplied number, not derived from conductivity and film thickness. It is irrelevant to this question and should not be mistaken for a blocker.
- The module-level `sheet_impedance.COSTA_EQ10_FORM` switch (`"eps0"` vs `"eps0_epsr"`, #234) is a global string constant read at call time, not an optimization variable — nobody is proposing to make *which published equation to trust* a thing gradient descent tunes, so its `if form == "eps0":` branch does not enter the gradient path with respect to the continuous geometry parameters (`eps_r`, `tan_delta`, `thickness_m`, `period_m`, `gap_m`, `sheet_resistance_ohm_sq`, `squares`) at all.

**What is missing is not differentiability of the physics — it is a differentiable *implementation*.** The code as written calls Python's `math`/`cmath` modules on plain `float`/`complex` objects, which carry no computational graph and therefore give no gradient however smooth the underlying function is. JAX's `jax.numpy` module mirrors `math`/`cmath`'s API closely enough (`jnp.sqrt`, `jnp.tan`, `jnp.sin`, `jnp.log`, `jnp.log1p`, `jnp.exp`, native complex-dtype arrays) that the port is close to a search-and-replace, not a re-derivation — every equation in the docstrings stays exactly as published, only the library computing it changes. `absorptivity` is a real-valued function (`1 - |Gamma|^2`) built from complex-valued intermediates; JAX's non-holomorphic `jax.grad` handles exactly this shape (a real scalar output differentiated with respect to real inputs, with complex arithmetic in between) without special handling. PyTorch's `torch.autograd` has supported complex tensors since 1.9 and would also work, but JAX's `jnp` naming and functional style is the closer match to this codebase's existing pure-function style (`rf_tools/absorber.py`'s functions are already stateless and side-effect-free). The project's current dependencies (`pyproject.toml`: `numpy>=2.0`, `scikit-rf>=1.8`) include neither JAX nor PyTorch today, so this is a genuinely new dependency the ticket's "new ML dependencies are authorized" line is what clears.

## 3. Grounding the three candidate classes in primary sources

### 3a. Direct regression network trained on bulk-generated pairs

This is the shape the repo has already priced (§1): a network mapping geometry → response (or the inverse, response → geometry), trained on a large synthetic corpus. The canonical statement of *why this alone is insufficient* for inverse design specifically (response → geometry, one-to-many) comes from Liu, Tan, Khoram & Yu, **"Training Deep Neural Networks for the Inverse Design of Nanophotonic Structures,"** ACS Photonics 5(4):1365–1369 (2018), [arXiv:1710.04724](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.04724): *"by combining forward modeling and inverse design in a tandem architecture, one can overcome this fundamental issue"* of non-uniqueness — i.e. a **plain** regression network trained response→geometry does not, on its own, resolve the many-geometries-one-response problem; it needs to be paired with a second, forward-modeling network (a "tandem" architecture) precisely because the plain regression network alone will average over or arbitrarily pick among conflicting training targets. For `ABSORBER`, training this pairing costs real engineering (two networks, a training curriculum, hyperparameters) to recreate a mapping we can already evaluate exactly and, per §2, differentiate exactly — the tandem network's own "forward-modeling" half would just be a second, approximate copy of `absorber_band_response`.

### 3b. Conditional generative model (GAN / diffusion / VAE)

This class exists specifically to answer non-uniqueness with *diversity* rather than a single best guess. Two directly on-point, absorber-specific 2026 preprints confirm the shape and its stated motivation:

- A physics-guided conditional diffusion model for metasurface absorbers explicitly targets this: *"the model generates multiple geometries for the same condition, thereby providing diverse design alternatives to the engineer,"* addressing non-uniqueness by returning several candidates per target spectrum rather than one ([arxiv:2605.19611](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19611), via secondary summary — not read first-hand here, flagged `LITERATURE-SUPPORTED` at one remove pending a direct read).
- A GAN-based absorber inverse-design paper, "Inverse Design of Realizable Metasurface based Absorbers using Improved Conditioning and Diversity Enhanced Progressively Growing GANs" ([arXiv:2606.05849](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05849)), targets the same diversity-plus-manufacturability goal by title alone; not read first-hand here either.

Both are genuine answers to non-uniqueness, and both cost a training corpus, a generative-model training run, and calibration against fabrication constraints — the same cost structure §1 already priced for the survey's Example 3/7 cases, just cheaper to *generate* data for here because the forward model is closed-form. That "cheaper to generate data" point is real and worth stating plainly: it is the one way this ticket's premise (a free bulk-data generator) *does* change the usual calculus against generative models. But it does not close the gap against §3c below, because a generative model's core service — surfacing several structurally different candidates for one target — is exactly what running gradient descent from several different starting geometries against the *exact* physics already gives, at no training cost and with no approximation error, once the model is differentiable (§2). A generative model earns its keep when the forward model is too expensive or too opaque to search directly (a full-wave solver, a fabrication process); neither is true of `absorber_band_response`.

### 3c. Autodiff-based inversion of the closed-form model itself

This is not a hypothetical alternative — it is an established, named class of inverse design, and there is a paper doing almost exactly what §2 describes, on a closely analogous problem. Molesky, Lin, Piggott, Jin, Vučković & Rodriguez, **"Inverse design in nanophotonics,"** Nature Photonics 12:659–670 (2018), [arXiv:1801.06715](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.06715), is the standard review of gradient/adjoint-based inverse design that optimizes directly against a known physics model rather than a learned one:

> *"It is remarkable that adjoint methods yield full derivative information from the solution of a problem that is, in every respect, no harder to solve than the original problem"* — i.e. computing the gradient with respect to every design parameter costs about the same as one forward evaluation, regardless of how many parameters there are, versus finite-difference approaches that need one extra solve per parameter (*"Treating it in the forward direction requires as many solutions as there are optimization unknowns... Treating it in the reverse requires... the same factorization... used to solve for [the adjoint field]"*). It also states the honest limitation plainly: *"while there is no provable guarantee of a globally optimal solution... it is still nevertheless possible to find good (and in some cases globally optimal) designs"* — the non-uniqueness problem is handled by multiple starts/restarts across the design-space landscape, not eliminated, which is the same honest limitation a generative model has (it samples modes; it does not certify it found all of them, or the best one).

More directly on point — a **circuit-based closed-form model** used *instead of* a data-driven surrogate, specifically to avoid the training-data cost: Thakkar, Ruiz-García, Szymanski, Gok & Grbic, **"Inverse Design of Perfectly-Matched Metamaterials Via Circuit-Based Surrogate Models and the Adjoint Method,"** [arXiv:2505.01891](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.01891) (2025; read first-hand via the retrieved PDF text). The paper's own framing of the choice is the same one this ticket is asking:

> *"Alternatively, deep-learning-based strategies have been reported... where a convolutional neural network (CNN) rapidly predicts the scattering properties of an arbitrary pixelated geometry. However, this approach transfers the computational cost to training the CNN, where extensive data sets of accurate EM simulations are required. Instead, a computationally effective strategy was proposed... that avoids full-wave simulations in every iteration. The metamaterial was approximated as a circuit network, and its EM response rapidly evaluated using circuit theory... For these reasons, a fast 2-D circuit network solver (CNS) has been adopted in this work."*
>
> *"This allows the inverse-design scheme to rapidly and accurately solve the forward problem in every iteration. The gradient-based optimization is further accelerated by the adjoint variable method."*

That is `ABSORBER`'s situation almost exactly: a fast, closed-form (there, circuit-network; here, Costa/Luukkonen) forward model stands in for an expensive full-wave solve, and the paper's own stated reason for **not** training a neural surrogate on top of it is that doing so *"transfers the computational cost to training"* something that adds nothing the closed form didn't already give — echoing §1's finding independently, from an unrelated design family (a beamformer/collimator metamaterial, not an absorber). Their gradient is computed by a hand-derived adjoint-variable method rather than a general autodiff library, which is a legitimate implementation choice but not the only one; per §2, `absorber_band_response`'s math is simple enough (no PDE, no discretized field solve — a handful of closed-form complex-arithmetic expressions) that a general-purpose autodiff library (JAX) is the lower-effort route to the same gradient, with no hand-derived adjoint equations to maintain.

## 4. Recommendation

For `ABSORBER` under this ticket's stated conditions — a trusted closed-form forward model, cheap to bulk-evaluate, and (per §2) differentiable in principle with only a syntactic port required to make that usable — **the appropriate technique is direct, autodiff-based gradient optimization of the closed-form model itself, run from multiple random starting geometries to surface structurally different candidates**, not a network of either kind:

1. **A regression network adds nothing here and costs real things.** Its entire value elsewhere is amortizing an expensive forward evaluator (a full-wave solve, per the survey's own 7,000-to-80,000-solve data-cost ladder, §1) into a cheap one. `ABSORBER`'s forward evaluator is already cheap — training a network to approximate it is optimizing the wrong bottleneck, and per Liu et al. (§3a), a plain regression network doesn't even solve inverse design's real problem (non-uniqueness) without being paired into a tandem architecture, which is more engineering for no accuracy gain over the exact function it would be approximating.
2. **A conditional generative model is a genuine, literature-supported answer to non-uniqueness (§3b)** — but it answers the same question multi-start gradient descent against the exact model already answers, at the cost of a training pipeline this family does not need. It becomes the right tool once the forward model stops being cheap and exact — a full-wave-solved Tier B family, say, matching where ADR-0039 actually positions the "ML-direct third tier."
3. **Autodiff-based inversion is the class that matches this specific situation**, and it is a recognized, published approach (§3c) rather than a novel proposal — Thakkar et al. (2025) do it with a hand-derived adjoint on a circuit model; `ABSORBER`'s simpler, PDE-free closed form makes a general-purpose autodiff library (JAX, given its close `math`/`cmath` API match and native non-holomorphic complex-gradient support) a lower-effort route to the same gradient. The port needed is narrow and enumerable: swap `math.*`/`cmath.*` calls in `rf_tools/absorber.py` and the functions it calls in `rf_tools/sheet_impedance.py` for `jax.numpy` equivalents, replace the Python `for` loop over frequency points with `jax.vmap`, and either drop the input-validation guards inside the traced path or call them once on plain Python floats before entering it. No equation changes, because §2 found nothing in the published math that needs one.

**What this means for #559's own framing:** the honest answer to "what class of *ML* inverse-design technique is appropriate" is that the appropriate technique for `ABSORBER` is not, in the sense the ticket and ADR-0039 both mean by "ML," a *learned* model at all — it is applying an automatic-differentiation library as a differentiation tool to a physics model the project already trusts, wholly independent of ADR-0039's historical-data gate for its genuinely learned third tier. That gate stays exactly where ADR-0039 left it for families that lack a cheap, closed, trusted forward model — this finding does not touch it, and does not itself decide anything about the map issue #556's other families, several of which (per `docs/ai-metasurface-survey-against-the-seven-examples.md` §2's own tier split) are Tier B and do not have a `ABSORBER`-style closed form to differentiate.

## 5. What this does not settle

- **Not implemented.** No JAX/PyTorch port of `rf_tools/absorber.py` exists yet; this is a technique recommendation, not a landed change. Adding the dependency and writing the port is separate work.
- **Two of the three cited absorber/metamaterial papers were read only via secondary summary, not first-hand**: the diffusion paper (arXiv:2605.19611, §3b) and the GAN paper (arXiv:2606.05849, named but not opened at all). Molesky et al. (2018) and Thakkar et al. (2025) were both read first-hand from retrieved full text.
- **This does not reopen #234** (the disputed Costa eq. 10 prefactor) or any other open dispute inside `rf_tools/absorber.py`'s physics — an autodiff port would carry whichever form is selected at `sheet_impedance.COSTA_EQ10_FORM` forward unchanged, exactly as the existing closed-form callers do.
- **It does not decide `ABSORBER_TRANSMISSIVE` or any Tier B family's technique** — only `ABSORBER`'s own one-port, closed-form case was examined here, per the ticket's scope.
