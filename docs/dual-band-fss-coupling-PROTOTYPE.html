Dual-Band FSS Coupling Feel-Test

The question this answers: our research on stacking two differently-tuned resonant layers to build a GPS L1/L2 filter found that every published design which achieved two genuinely independent passbands did so only after adding a deliberate "decoupling" trick — and every one of those designs separated its two bands by roughly 1.85–2.4× in frequency. GPS L1 (1575.42 MHz) and L2 (1227.60 MHz) are only 1.283× apart — tighter than anything verified. This prototype lets you feel what "the bands blur together" actually looks like, and whether a decoupling knob can rescue a tight ratio like GPS's the way it rescued the wider ratios in the papers.

What this is not: this is a toy coupled-resonator circuit model (the same textbook physics — two resonant "tanks" linked by a coupling term — every paper in the research used to explain their own designs), not a full-wave simulation of any real geometry. Nothing here is SIMULATED or CALCULATED evidence about the actual GPS filter — it's a feel-it tool for the underlying idea, meant to surface "wait, that's surprising" moments before anyone spends real EM-solver time on it.
