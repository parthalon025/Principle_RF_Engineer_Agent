# Diverge to Screen, Converge to Commit: Sequencing the Issue #87 Design Loop

*A macro-level research synthesis for `parthalon025/Principle_RF_Engineer_Agent`, written in service of issue #87 ("Fast software iteration loop: prose requirements → scored candidates → batched lab test plan → external results") and tested against map #104 ("Printed metamaterial EM skin design loop") as the concrete applied case.*

## 0. Scope note: what is already decided, and what this document is for

Issue #87 is the governing spec. Its **Implementation Decisions** section already fixes the solver's scope, its convergence rules, and its gating. Its **Out of Scope** section states, as *already decided* rather than as an open question:

> "**Loosening any approval gate**, and **autonomous multi-candidate *architecture* search** — the solver searches parameters inside an approved architecture, not architectures themselves (ADR-0014 records the rejected alternative)."

That sentence is locked at the spec level, not merely at the ADR level — a stronger seal than ADR-0014 alone. Nothing below argues for reopening it. Where the prior-art research *would* push in that direction (real options, set-based concurrent engineering), this document says so explicitly and labels it "would require reopening issue #87 / ADR-0014" rather than folding it in as a quiet recommendation.

Map #104 is the applied test of every recommendation here: it is reproducing US12089385B2's Example 3 absorber against Landy et al.'s 2008 measured curve, with material *and* substrate as searched variables, fabrication limits as hard constraints, and unit-cell periodicity (not patch length) as the design variable. Two of its standing preferences matter throughout this document: **"the loop fills unknowns; it does not block on them"** (every unknown gets a provisional value at its true evidence tier, a human confirms or overrides later), and **"two operating modes, confirmation deferred not skipped"** (attended vs. unattended-overnight-with-morning-review).

## 1. The loop as built today

A recurring term below is **provenance** — a tag on every recorded value saying *how sure we are of it and how we got it*: `ASSUMED` (an LLM's or a default's guess, not yet human-confirmed), `CALCULATED` (deterministic arithmetic — no LLM judgment involved), `SIMULATED` (a simulator's output), and `MEASURED` (a real, physical bench result — the top of the trust ladder). Provenance is what lets a reader tell "97% of target" apart from "97% likely to satisfy the customer" when the target itself might still be an unconfirmed guess.

### 1.1 The nine steps — which are gated, which are automated, and the VERIFICATION question

`orchestration/design_loop.py` defines a 9-value `DesignStep` `StrEnum` (`design_loop.py:130–143`, confirmed by direct read): `REQUIREMENTS`, `ARCHITECTURE`, `ANALYSIS`, `SIMULATION`, `OPTIMIZATION`, `VERIFICATION`, `MEASUREMENT`, `CORRELATION`, `REDESIGN_DECISION`, walked in that order by `STEP_ORDER` (`design_loop.py:146–156`).

**`VERIFICATION` is real.** It is a genuine, distinct step at `design_loop.py:140` — not an old name, not an alias for `MEASUREMENT`, not a stale comment. `_handle_verification` (`design_loop.py:626–638`) compiles a verification-matrix entry (`requirement_id`, `requirement`, `method`, `status` — checked against `VERIFICATION_STATUSES`, `design_loop.py:165`) from evidence that was, conceptually, already recorded earlier in the loop. In plain terms: it writes down "here is what we checked and what the result was," it does not go and check anything itself.

**Two independent axes, easy to conflate, that the module keeps separate:**

- An *"implementation weight"* axis (module docstring, `design_loop.py:57–64`): `ARCHITECTURE`, `VERIFICATION`, and `REDESIGN_DECISION` are grouped as "lighter-weight recorded decisions" — steps whose handler just writes down a decision, rather than calling out to a Phase 1–11 calculation/simulation function.
- A *"requires human approval"* axis: `GATED_STEPS = frozenset({ARCHITECTURE, MEASUREMENT, REDESIGN_DECISION})` (`design_loop.py:160–162`, confirmed by direct read).

**These do not overlap the way a skim would suggest.** `VERIFICATION` is lighter-weight (no external function call, `design_loop.py:626–638`) but is **not** gated. `MEASUREMENT` **is** gated but *is* a deep tool integration — it calls `measurement.external.record_external_measurement` (`design_loop.py:664–668`). Reading the docstring's grouping as implying `VERIFICATION` requires approval would be an incorrect inference the code directly contradicts. In plain terms: "how much code this step calls" and "does a human have to sign off on it" are two separate questions, and the loop answers them independently for each step.

The full step table:

| Step | Gated? | What it actually does | Handler |
|---|---|---|---|
| `REQUIREMENTS` | — (produced by `start_design_loop`, not advanced via `advance_loop_step`) | Validates the prose requirement dict is non-empty, records it as an `ASSUMED`-provenance decision, seeds a new loop at `iteration=1`, `current_step=ARCHITECTURE` | `design_loop.py:347–380` |
| `ARCHITECTURE` | **Yes** | Records a human/agent-authored decision + rationale + required `design_family` (issue #161) | `design_loop.py:421–435` |
| `ANALYSIS` | No | Calculates patch resonant frequency, at one `eps_r` or at both ends of an `eps_r` bracket | `design_loop.py:497–527`, `_resolve_eps_r_bounds` at `438–494` |
| `SIMULATION` | No | Runs NEC2++ at one frequency; derives VSWR/return-loss/reflection-coefficient against an explicit reference impedance | `design_loop.py:530–602` |
| `OPTIMIZATION` | No | Searches patch length for a target frequency (Bayesian by default, method-overridable) | `design_loop.py:605–623` |
| `VERIFICATION` | No | Compiles a verification-matrix record from already-recorded evidence | `design_loop.py:626–638` |
| `MEASUREMENT` | **Yes** | Records an externally-obtained Touchstone file — no live-instrument path exists at all | `design_loop.py:641–669` |
| `CORRELATION` | No | Compares simulated vs. measured S-parameters | `design_loop.py:703–733` |
| `REDESIGN_DECISION` | **Yes** | Records the iterate-vs-accept human judgment that closes or loops the iteration | `design_loop.py:736–748` |

(In plain terms, the three numbers `SIMULATION` derives — VSWR, return loss, and reflection coefficient — are three ways of stating the same physical fact: how much of the signal reflects back off the antenna instead of being accepted/radiated, at the one frequency point NEC2++ was asked about. Reflection coefficient is the raw signed/complex fraction reflected; return loss restates that in decibels — a bigger negative-dB number is a better match; VSWR restates it again as the ratio engineers historically read off a bench meter. None of the three carries information the others lack; which one a given requirement is stated in is convention, not physics.)

One structural oddity worth flagging plainly: `MEASUREMENT` is the only advanceable step *omitted* from the `_STEP_HANDLERS` dispatch dict (`design_loop.py:751–759`) — it is special-cased directly inside `advance_loop_step` (`799–800`) instead. Nothing in the file documents why; its handler signature is otherwise structurally identical to, e.g., `_handle_correlation` (which *is* in the dict and also reads `state`). Flagged as an unexplained implementation-history artifact, not a design decision.

### 1.2 The dependency graph: who actually reads prior state, and who doesn't

This is the single most load-bearing fact for anything downstream in this document. Despite reading like a linear nine-step pipeline, **only one step — `CORRELATION` — actually reads earlier steps' results out of `DesignLoopState` programmatically.** It does so via `_find_last_decision(state, SIMULATION)` (`design_loop.py:709`) and `_find_last_decision(state, MEASUREMENT)` (`714`), with an explicit `step_input['simulated']`/`['measured']` override escape hatch (`711–715`).

Every other handler's `state` parameter is either underscore-prefixed (unused) or, in `_handle_measurement`, explicitly `del`eted with a docstring explaining that measurement recording is deliberately stateless (`design_loop.py:654–662`). Concretely: `REQUIREMENTS`, `ARCHITECTURE`, `ANALYSIS`, `SIMULATION`, `OPTIMIZATION`, `VERIFICATION`, and `REDESIGN_DECISION` **all require the caller to manually re-supply whatever earlier values matter, in `step_input`.** Nothing auto-flows between them in code. In plain terms: the state machine remembers *that* something happened at each step (a decision gets appended to a list), but it only actively *reads that memory back* in one place — everywhere else, whoever calls `advance_loop_step` has to carry the relevant numbers forward by hand.

### 1.3 The one feedback loop: `REDESIGN_DECISION` → `ARCHITECTURE`

`REDESIGN_DECISION`'s `next_action` has exactly two legal values (`REDESIGN_ACTIONS = {"iterate", "accept_design"}`, `design_loop.py:171`):

- **`"iterate"`** (`831–840`): bumps `state.iteration` by 1, resets `current_step` to `ARCHITECTURE.value`, sets `completed=False`, and *appends* (never clears) `decisions` — so every prior iteration's decision history, the requirements dict, `loop_id`, and `created_at` all survive; only `current_step` is reset.
- **`"accept_design"`** (`841–846`): sets `completed=True`; `current_step` stays at `REDESIGN_DECISION.value`; this is terminal — any further `advance_loop_step` call raises `OrchestrationError` (`781–787`).

This is the loop's *only* cyclic edge. Section 3 applies Design Structure Matrix theory to this fact directly.

### 1.4 The two convergence mechanisms that already exist (in `orchestration/solver.py`)

`run_candidate_search` (`solver.py:857–1172`) drives a caller-supplied **batch** of LLM-proposed candidates, one at a time in list order, through the design loop's already-ungated `ANALYSIS → SIMULATION → OPTIMIZATION` span (`_ORDERED_UNGATED_SPAN`, `solver.py:405–409`, confirmed by direct read — it deliberately excludes `CORRELATION`, for the reason explained in §1.5).

1. **`worst_of_scored_steps`** (per candidate): `overall_score_percent` = the **minimum** of that candidate's scored steps' `score_percent` (`_overall_score`, `solver.py:727–742`). In plain terms: a candidate is only as good as its *worst*-performing requirement — not its average, not its best. This rejects "best single score" (hides a failing requirement behind a passing one) and "mean/average" (a below-threshold gain and an above-threshold frequency could average to something comfortable while neither is actually satisfied) — both explicitly argued and rejected in the module's own ~380-line docstring.
2. **`score_plateau`** (across the batch): a running list of the best-seen `overall_score_percent` so far; once it holds `plateau_window + 1` entries, if the latest value beats the one `plateau_window` entries back by no more than `plateau_epsilon` percentage points, the search stops (`solver.py:1147–1163`, confirmed by direct read). Defaults: `plateau_window=5`, `plateau_epsilon=0.5` (both caller-overridable). In plain terms: five candidates in a row whose *best-so-far* score has moved less than half a percentage point is read as "this batch has stopped improving."

Two more stop conditions exist alongside these: `target_satisfaction_threshold` (default 100.0; the *first* candidate to clear it ends the search immediately, `solver.py:1139–1145`, confirmed by direct read) and `evaluation_budget` exhaustion (the for-loop's `else` clause, `1164–1168`, confirmed by direct read).

### 1.5 What issue #87 and ADR-0014 already rule out

Issue #87's Out of Scope section (quoted in full above, §0) and ADR-0014 both foreclose, as decided: (a) loosening any of the three gates (`ARCHITECTURE`, `MEASUREMENT`, `REDESIGN_DECISION`), and (b) autonomous multi-candidate *architecture* search — trying several architectures and auto-picking a winner before a human sees the rejected ones. `solver.py`'s own docstring (lines 64–70, per ground truth) reaffirms this for the solver module specifically: a state sitting at `ARCHITECTURE` is refused with `stop_reason='gated_step_pending_approval'` and zero candidates evaluated, before any search happens at all.

`CORRELATION`'s exclusion from the solver's driven span is a *correction*, not an oversight: ADR-0014's original prose named `ANALYSIS/SIMULATION/OPTIMIZATION/CORRELATION`, but issue #95's acceptance criteria narrowed it to three, because `CORRELATION` needs a recorded `MEASUREMENT` decision — which is gated — so driving it automatically would either require the very lab trip the solver exists to defer, or accept a caller-supplied `measured=` override with no real gated `MEASUREMENT` decision behind it: "a softer, easier-to-miss version of exactly the gate-bypass ADR-0014 forbids" (`solver.py` docstring, per ground truth). ADR-0014 was itself amended to record this correction rather than leave it looking like a dropped requirement.

## 2. Where the loop should diverge vs. converge, step by step

"Diverge" here means: keep multiple candidates alive and let the search explore broadly. "Converge" means: commit to one value and move on. Below, each step/transition is graded against the prior-art findings, with explicit citation of which finding backs which call — and explicit disagreement noted where the literature doesn't cleanly resolve the question.

### `REQUIREMENTS` — converge (already decided by issue #87, not a live question)

Issue #87's own user stories (8–14) already specify exactly one LLM-proposed interpretation of the prose requirement, tagged `ASSUMED`, confirmed or corrected by a human before any scoring happens. This is not a search problem — there is no "propose three readings of the prose and pick the best-scoring one" step anywhere in the design, and none is recommended here. This matches Cooper's Stage-Gate framing (1990): exploration happens inside a stage, the gate is where commitment happens — here the "stage" is the LLM's proposal, the "gate" is the human confirmation the issue already specifies.

### `ARCHITECTURE` — converge, hard (the locked gate)

This is where issue #87's Out-of-Scope lock and ADR-0014 bind directly: exactly one architecture is committed per iteration, by a human, before anything else runs. The Neural Architecture Search literature (§5c below) is the single strongest piece of supporting evidence that this is the *right* default, not merely the mandated one: even ML's own most efficiency-obsessed automated-architecture-search methods remain expensive or narrow relative to ordinary training (Zoph & Le 2017; Real, Aggarwal, Huang & Le 2019), and the field's own reproducibility studies found much of automated search's apparent benefit is confounded by a human-designed scaffold and evaluation-protocol tricks (Li & Talwalkar 2019; Yang, Esperança & Carlucci 2020). A single RF/EM simulation here is far more expensive per candidate than a CIFAR-10 forward pass — this repo's domain is *worse*-suited to auto-search than NAS's own home turf, and NAS's own field hasn't fully committed to automating this either (Elsken, Metzen & Hutter 2019).

**Where prior art disagrees, and is flagged rather than folded in:** Real-options theory (Myers 1977; Dixit & Pindyck 1994) frames a partially-irreversible commitment under uncertainty as something whose value comes precisely from deferring it — by that logic, keeping several candidate architectures alive longer, before committing, could be worth more than committing early. Set-Based Concurrent Engineering (Ward, Liker, Cristiano & Sobek 1995; Sobek, Ward & Liker 1999) is a whole discipline built on exactly this: carry a *set* of design alternatives forward, narrow by eliminating infeasible regions, commit only at the last responsible moment — and it is *genuinely absent* from this codebase (`DesignLoopState` carries exactly one architecture decision per iteration; the only SBCE-adjacent code is the material-property bracket handling in `_resolve_eps_r_bounds`, `design_loop.py:438–494`, which carries a *range* through one input, not through the architecture decision itself). ADR-0014's own docstring already considered and rejected exactly this move — "auto-trying multiple architectures... auto-pick the best-scoring one before a human ever sees the rejected ones" — for a stated reason (the project's "never allow autonomous manufacturing release" principle is the single most load-bearing rule in the codebase, and a new tool making design work faster is not sufficient reason to carve the first exception into it). Real options and SBCE do not refute that reason; they simply name a genuine tension the ADR consciously accepted, not resolved. **Pursuing multi-architecture divergence would require reopening issue #87's Out-of-Scope clause and ADR-0014 — not recommended here, logged as fog in §7.**

### `ANALYSIS` — diverge (the cheap screening tier)

This is the right place to run many candidates cheaply, because the calculation is cheap (a closed-form patch-resonant-frequency calculation today, per `design_loop.py:497–527`; a per-shape-family scattering surrogate for map #104's metamaterial case). Three independent literatures converge on the same shape here:

- **Hardware design-space exploration (DSE)**: Chavet, Coussy, Urard & Martin (arXiv:0706.1692) formalize automatically regenerating and re-evaluating candidate architectures under varied constraints rather than restarting by hand for each one — structurally the same move as batch-evaluating candidates through `ANALYSIS`.
- **Response-surface / surrogate modeling**: Box & Wilson (1951) established "don't grid an expensive response, walk it sequentially, informed by a cheap local approximation"; Sacks, Welch, Mitchell & Wynn (1989) and Marwala (arXiv:0705.1759, a concrete FE-model surrogate that beat direct-GA-on-the-real-simulator by ~10,000-evaluations'-worth of cost) give the quantified case for fitting something cheap before searching broadly.
- **Bounded Pareto-archive convergence**: Laumanns (arXiv:0711.2949) proves convergence-with-probability-one for random search that maintains a fixed-size archive of non-dominated candidates via a grid-based admission rule — the formal backing for "keep a diverse set of survivors alive, narrow gradually" rather than collapsing to one candidate immediately. (In plain terms: a "non-dominated" candidate is one that isn't strictly worse than another surviving candidate on every score at once — the archive keeps a handful of different good-in-different-ways designs alive rather than collapsing early to whichever one happens to be winning on today's single number.)

Map #104's own already-decided architecture already implements exactly this shape: a cheap effective-medium/closed-form scattering surrogate (one Floquet solve per shape family — simulating one repeating unit cell with periodic boundary conditions standing in for the whole infinite sheet, rather than modeling the entire sheet directly — then algebra) explores broadly, and only top-scoring, **diversity-filtered** survivors are promoted to the expensive tier. That diversity-filtering step is precisely Laumanns' archive-admission rule in practice, whether or not the code names it that way.

### `SIMULATION` — converge relative to `ANALYSIS` (the narrowing step)

Map #104's own decision here (full-wave Floquet confirmation on two independent solvers — Meep-extended and EMerge, after Palace/openEMS were found structurally incapable) is where the funnel narrows: only the survivors that passed `ANALYSIS`'s screen get this expensive treatment. This is the Pareto-archive narrowing (Laumanns 2007) applied at the boundary between the cheap and expensive tiers, and it is also structurally the same shape as the pharmaceutical discovery funnel (Paul, Mytelka, Dunwiddie, Persinger, Munos, Lindborg & Schacht 2010): push cheap, fast screening as far as it can go before paying for the expensive, slow step — with the honest caveat that the drug pipeline has *several* successive narrowing gates across months-to-years, while this repo has one funnel-narrowing event (the solver's batch-to-survivors cut) feeding one expensive confirmation tier per iteration — don't over-claim a multi-gate match that isn't there.

`design_loop.py`'s own `SIMULATION` handler (NEC2++, `530–602`) is *also* a genuinely expensive per-candidate evaluation (up to a 600-second timeout per single-frequency call, `single_frequency_prediction=True`) — this is the real "expensive black-box function" case the Efficient Global Optimization literature (Jones, Schonlau & Welch 1998) exists for, and it is *not yet* where this repo's existing Bayesian-optimization machinery is pointed (see §4).

### `OPTIMIZATION` — converge on a value, but the *proposal strategy* should be guided, not blind

Issue #87 locks this step's scope explicitly: "parameter-level search within an already-approved architecture." Within that lock, `optimization/bayesian.py` already implements exactly the recipe the literature recommends — it cites Jones, Schonlau & Welch (1998) by name, runs a Gaussian-process fit plus Expected-Improvement acquisition (the formal machinery from Sacks, Welch, Mitchell & Wynn 1989 and Jones et al. 1998), as `OPTIMIZATION`'s default method (`design_loop.py:620`, `method="bayesian"`, `n_evaluations=20` default). (In plain terms: rather than testing evenly-spaced values across the whole range — a grid — or guessing at random, this method keeps a running best-guess curve of how the score probably varies with the parameter, plus how uncertain that guess still is at each point, and proposes the next value to try wherever that curve says a big improvement is most plausible — a high average guess, or a highly uncertain one worth a look. That running best-guess curve is the "Gaussian process"; the rule for picking the next point from it is "Expected Improvement.") Wolpert & Rajnarayan (arXiv:0704.1274) supply the general first-principles justification for exactly this move — treat the unknown objective as a posterior-updated random variable and let the posterior decide where to look next, rather than gridding blindly, whenever each real evaluation is costly.

The honest caveat, already present in the prior-art research: the objective this machinery currently searches (`patch_resonant_frequency_hz`) is a *closed-form calculation*, not genuinely expensive — so EGO's actual payoff (minimizing costly evaluations) is banked, not yet realized, for the currently-fast steps. The genuinely expensive evaluations in this repo today are `SIMULATION` (NEC2++, up to 600s/point) and `MEASUREMENT` (gated, physical). Neither currently uses a sequential/adaptive design. **Recommendation, developed fully in §4 and §7: point the existing EGO/Bayesian machinery at the expensive `SIMULATION`-tier objective once map #104's full-wave Floquet solve is wired into the driven span, rather than reserving it only for the cheap closed-form objective it currently searches.**

**Where prior art does *not* apply, and should not be forced:** bandit theory (Auer, Cesa-Bianchi & Fischer 2002 — UCB1; Thompson 1933) and bandit-based hyperparameter search (Hyperband — Li, Jamieson, DeSalvo, Rostamizadeh & Talwalkar 2018; BOHB — Falkner, Klein & Hutter 2018) are algorithms for *revisiting and re-weighting* options across many rounds using feedback from prior pulls. `solver.py` does none of this — candidates arrive as one flat, LLM-proposed batch, evaluated exactly once each, with no posterior, no confidence bound, and no re-pulling (`solver.py` docstring, Design Question 1: "a one-candidate-per-call shape would put a full LLM round trip between every evaluation, reintroducing exactly the bottleneck ... this ticket exists to remove"). The only place these share real DNA with `solver.py` is at the level of *stopping-rule* shape (batch-then-prune, diminishing-returns halt, budget cap) — not candidate-selection mechanism. Do not recommend restructuring `solver.py` into a bandit algorithm.

### `VERIFICATION` — converge trivially (no search happens here at all)

`_handle_verification` compiles a record from evidence already recorded elsewhere (`design_loop.py:626–638`); it is not a decision point in any search sense. Al-Shami, Malik, Ala-Laurinaho, Vepsalainen & Viitala (arXiv:2606.23395) — a SysML v2 human-in-the-loop framework that keeps an LLM's output "diffable" for cheap human review rather than a full rewrite — supports keeping this step's output small and inspectable, which it already is by construction.

### `MEASUREMENT` — converge, hard (already locked; here is why it must stay a real gate)

Exactly one external measurement per iteration, gated (ADR-0013, issue #87 Implementation Decisions). Ashby's Law of Requisite Variety (Ashby 1956, pp. 202–218) supplies the clean, non-forced justification for *why*: a regulator (here, the RF/EM simulator) can only ever cancel as much variety (distinguishable-outcome complexity) as it itself contains — "only variety can absorb variety." NEC2++ or any EM simulator has strictly bounded variety; it can only represent the physics it was built to model, at the fidelity it was given. The real fabricated device — material variation, manufacturing tolerance, unmodeled coupling — carries *more* variety than any simulator can represent. By Ashby's law, no amount of simulate-and-optimize can substitute for a channel that carries the real system's actual variety back into the loop. That channel is `MEASUREMENT`, and it is exactly why `solver.py`'s docstring insists `CORRELATION` cannot be faked: a recorded `MEASUREMENT` decision cannot exist without having already passed the gate.

### `CORRELATION` — inherently single-valued, no divergence question applies

`CORRELATION` compares exactly one simulated result against exactly one measured result (`design_loop.py:703–733`) — there is nothing to diverge across here structurally. The genuinely interesting question about `CORRELATION` is not divergence/convergence but whether its error signal should behave like backpropagation's — addressed fully in §5b, with the answer being: only partially, and the analogy must not be pushed past "quantify the miss, don't eyeball it."

### `REDESIGN_DECISION` — converge (the gate that closes the loop) — but the *tearing point* it always resets to is worth questioning with evidence, not assumption

This is Cooper's Stage-Gate (1990), Forsberg & Mooz's V-model (1991), and the NASA SE Handbook's (2016) reviewed-checkpoint pattern lining up almost exactly with `GATED_STEPS`: three human go/no-go points bracketing ungated calculation/simulation work. Boehm's spiral model (1988) describes what `REDESIGN_DECISION`'s iterate/accept choice already does at the whole-loop level — one iteration is one risk-retiring cycle — but its finer-grained idea (each cycle sizing its *own* scope to whichever risk is currently biggest) is not present: `STEP_ORDER` runs the same fixed nine-step sequence every iteration regardless of what the prior `REDESIGN_DECISION` actually flagged as the open risk.

The concrete, checkable finding here (developed fully in §3): `design_loop.py`'s `"iterate"` branch *always* tears back to `ARCHITECTURE` — the longest possible feedback path. That is the loop's own step machine; the *persisted* record of the same event lives one layer up, in `designs.status`, and reads differently. ADR-0011 already settled how: `orchestration/tooling.py:494`'s `final_status = DesignStatus.ANALYSIS if next_action == "iterate" else DesignStatus.PASS` (confirmed by direct read) rounds every `"iterate"` up to `designs.status=ANALYSIS`, regardless of how far back the loop's own `current_step` actually tore. `designs/lifecycle.py`'s `LEGAL_TRANSITIONS` tear to `ANALYSIS` on `FAIL`/`BLOCKED`/`CONDITIONAL_PASS` (`designs/lifecycle.py:66–68`, confirmed by direct read: `FAIL: {ANALYSIS, BLOCKED}`, `BLOCKED: {ANALYSIS, FAIL}`, `CONDITIONAL_PASS: {RELEASED, ANALYSIS, FAIL, BLOCKED}`) is a *different* event, reachable only through the separate, general-purpose `update_design_status` tool (`agent/main.py:1632`), which needs no design-loop iteration running at all — and `DesignStatus` (`designs/models.py`) has no `ARCHITECTURE` member for it to tear back to even if it wanted to. So this is not two state machines disagreeing on one shared question; it is one documented mapping (ADR-0011) plus one separately-triggered, coarser vocabulary that cannot express the distinction the loop's own tear preserves. The real, open question this leaves: `designs.status=ANALYSIS` reads identically whether the underlying `"iterate"` redid the whole architecture or only tweaked a parameter under the same one — is that collapse a deliberate simplification, or a real information loss for whoever reads `designs.status` without also reading the loop's own decision trail? Developed concretely in §3; a narrower ticket than a first pass through this material would suggest, in §7, item 4.

## 3. Dependency model: applying Design Structure Matrix thinking to the actual steps

**Is this a simple chain, or does it have real feedback loops?** Both, and the ground truth pins down exactly where each applies.

Steward's original Design Structure System (Steward 1981) represents a design's tasks as a matrix of who-needs-what-from-whom; a matrix that is strictly lower-triangular (every task depends only on earlier tasks) can run straight through once, and any matrix entry *above* the diagonal is a "circuit" — a feedback mark that forces iteration and must be planned for, not discovered mid-project.

Applying this directly to the nine named steps: `STEP_ORDER`'s `REQUIREMENTS → ARCHITECTURE → ANALYSIS → SIMULATION → OPTIMIZATION → VERIFICATION → MEASUREMENT → CORRELATION → REDESIGN_DECISION` is **already exactly Steward's triangularized case for a single pass**. The one genuinely programmatic backward reference — `CORRELATION` reading `SIMULATION` and `MEASUREMENT` (`design_loop.py:709,714`) — is still a within-pass, backward-but-not-cyclic reference: it reads *earlier* steps in the *same* iteration, it does not loop anything back. The **single feedback mark in the whole system** is `REDESIGN_DECISION`'s `"iterate"` edge back to `ARCHITECTURE` (`design_loop.py:831–840`) — one Stewardian circuit, already isolated at the very end of the chain. That is close to the textbook-correct placement: gather all evidence (simulation, measurement, correlation) before the one unavoidable feedback point, rather than scattering iteration triggers throughout.

**Where DSM reveals a concrete, actionable finding:** `design_loop.py`'s `REDESIGN_DECISION` tears its own `current_step` to `ARCHITECTURE` on every `"iterate"` (`design_loop.py:836`) — the single Stewardian circuit in the loop's own dependency structure, per the paragraph above. The persisted record of that same event, however, is coarser: ADR-0011 rounds it to `designs.status=ANALYSIS` (`orchestration/tooling.py:494`, confirmed by direct read), and `designs/lifecycle.py`'s own `LEGAL_TRANSITIONS` (`designs/lifecycle.py:66–68`) reaches that same `ANALYSIS` value on `FAIL`/`BLOCKED`/`CONDITIONAL_PASS` through an entirely different, separately-triggered mechanism (the general-purpose `update_design_status` tool, `agent/main.py:1632`, usable with no design loop running at all). `DesignStatus` (`designs/models.py`) has no `ARCHITECTURE` member, so `LEGAL_TRANSITIONS` was never choosing a *shorter* tear as an alternative to `design_loop.py`'s own — it has no vocabulary to express that tear in the first place. Eppinger & Browning's (2012) tearing heuristic — break the feedback mark whose broken dependency is cheapest to re-estimate, decided from evidence about the actual coupling, not asserted — therefore applies to a narrower question than "which of two tears is right": whether `designs.status`'s rounding-up of every `"iterate"` to `ANALYSIS`, regardless of whether the loop's own tear went all the way back to `ARCHITECTURE` or not, is losing real information a reader of `designs.status` alone would want. **This is not a recommendation to add a second, shorter reentry point to `REDESIGN_DECISION` itself — the two mechanisms are not actually competing for that role.** It is a recommendation to first *collect the evidence* Eppinger & Browning's method needs: does an `"iterate"` decision's own rationale field, in practice, ever reference only an `OPTIMIZATION`-level tweak rather than the architecture itself, and if the two cases are common and worth distinguishing, does `DesignStatus` need a finer value rather than silently collapsing them? See §7's secondary list, item 4.

**Where DSM reveals a real, currently-unaddressed opportunity — but not one to act on yet:** `solver.py`'s candidate-evaluation loop (`1091`) is a plain sequential `for i in range(effective_budget):` — confirmed no `concurrent`/`parallel`/`thread`/`async` machinery anywhere in the file. Each candidate forks fresh from the *same* unmutated `base_state` (guaranteed by `design_loop.py:767–768`: `advance_loop_step` never mutates its input) — so nothing about any one candidate's own drive through `ANALYSIS → SIMULATION → OPTIMIZATION` requires sequential execution relative to any *other* candidate. Framed as a DSM decomposition: this is a fully decomposable, off-diagonal block — `N` independent candidate-drives with zero coupling among them. But the two score-based stopping rules (`target_satisfaction`'s early `break`, `score_plateau`'s running-best-over-a-window) are explicitly *defined on evaluation order* ("running best... in evaluation order"; "over the last `plateau_window`... successfully-evaluated candidates") — that bookkeeping is a genuine sequential dependency, proven load-bearing by the test that places a target-satisfying candidate before an off-target one and shows the third is never reached. A DSM-correct restructuring, if this were ever pursued, would tear these into **two separate matrices**: (1) the `N` candidate-drives themselves, fully parallelizable, and (2) a strictly sequential "stop-rule arbiter" that consumes results **in the original candidate-list order** (even if the drives complete out of order) and applies `target_satisfaction`/`score_plateau` bookkeeping serially over that fixed order. This is not recommended for immediate implementation — no performance problem has been reported, and today's driven objective (`ANALYSIS`/`OPTIMIZATION` against a closed-form calculation) is cheap enough that sequential evaluation costs nothing meaningful. It becomes relevant exactly when map #104's full-wave `SIMULATION` tier (Meep-extended + EMerge, genuinely slow per candidate) is wired into the driven span. Logged as fog in §7, item 8.

## 4. Solving for "the final solution"

Issue #87's user stories are explicit that the fast loop's job is to converge *as far as software can take it*, then hand off — the macro question ("is this design actually done") is different from, and sits above, `solver.py`'s own per-batch stopping rules.

**What "done" means at the macro level, and why it is largely already the architecture, just undocumented as a stated principle:** `REDESIGN_DECISION`'s `"accept_design"` branch (`design_loop.py:171,841–846`) is a human, gated, terminal decision with no code-enforced rubric beyond `REDESIGN_ACTIONS`'s two legal values. Herbert Simon's satisficing (Simon 1969, *The Sciences of the Artificial*) is the cleanest available frame for what should govern that human's judgment: real, bounded agents search alternatives only until one clears a stated aspiration level, then stop — they do not keep searching for a provably-best option once "good enough" is reached, and the aspiration level itself gets revised (usually downward) when continued search evidently is not paying off. This maps almost exactly onto `solver.py`'s own `target_satisfaction_threshold` (an explicit aspiration level; the search stops the instant a candidate clears it) and `score_plateau` (stop when the evidence shows the alternative space isn't yielding more) — but Simon's frame belongs one level up, at `REDESIGN_DECISION`, as the explicit *macro* completion doctrine: a design is done when its confirmed requirement targets are cleared **at whatever evidence tier was actually achieved**, and `CORRELATION`'s simulated-vs-measured delta is the signal that decides whether that evidence tier is trustworthy enough to accept, or whether another calibration cycle is warranted. This matches map #104's own standing preference verbatim: "the loop fills unknowns; it does not block on them."

**A genuine, currently unaddressed gap surfaced by asking this question directly:** nothing in `REDESIGN_ACTIONS`/`_handle_redesign_decision` (`design_loop.py:736–748`) requires that `accept_design` ever be preceded by even one `MEASURED`-provenance `CORRELATION` decision. A design could, today, be accepted on `SIMULATED`-only evidence purely by human judgment, with no code-level floor. This is not the same question as "loosening a gate" (issue #87's Out-of-Scope item) — it would, if anything, *tighten* the bar for one specific action — but before treating it as a clean addition, check it against map #104 itself, not only against #87's Out-of-Scope wording: issue #133 already found map #104 has **no VNA and no measurement fixture available** at all, capping everything map #104 can itself produce at `SIMULATED` (map #104's own header caveat). A hard `MEASURED` floor, adopted as stated, would make map #104's own applied test case permanently unable to reach `accept_design` — developed fully in §7, item 5, where the recommendation is revised accordingly rather than dropped.

**Candidate-proposal strategy inside `OPTIMIZATION`:** the prior art gives a clear, non-forced answer, already partly implemented. Bayesian-optimization-style guided proposal (Expected Improvement over a Gaussian-process posterior — Sacks, Welch, Mitchell & Wynn 1989; Jones, Schonlau & Welch 1998) beats blind grid/random search whenever each real evaluation is costly — which is exactly the situation once map #104's full-wave Floquet solve becomes the objective being searched. `optimization/bayesian.py` already implements this recipe (citing Jones et al. 1998 by name) as `OPTIMIZATION`'s default method; the concrete recommendation is to extend the *same* machinery to the genuinely expensive `SIMULATION` tier once it exists for metamaterial candidates, rather than building a separate mechanism. This stays entirely within issue #87's locked scope — it is still parameter-level search inside one approved architecture, never architecture search.

## 5. Comparison to a neural network's training dynamics

### 5a. `worst_of_scored_steps` + `score_plateau` vs. gradient-descent convergence and early stopping

**This is not a loose metaphor — it is, parameter for parameter, the same mechanism as the field's dominant early-stopping method.** Prechelt (1998) formalized "patience"-based early stopping: monitor a validation metric, tolerate no-improvement for a fixed number of epochs (*patience*), and treat any change smaller than a minimum threshold (*min_delta*) as noise rather than progress. `solver.py`'s `score_plateau` rule maps onto this exactly: `plateau_window` = patience, `plateau_epsilon` = min_delta, the running-best `overall_score_percent` = the monitored metric. Prechelt's own 1,296-run empirical study found longer-patience criteria buy roughly a 4% average generalization improvement at roughly 4× the compute cost — i.e., patience trades compute for a small, diminishing gain, not a free win, which argues the specific defaults here (`plateau_window=5`, `plateau_epsilon=0.5` percentage points — five candidates, half-a-point-of-wiggle-room) are a reasonable, caller-overridable default rather than an obviously wrong one. **Verdict: ML practice validates this rule's basic shape.**

**One real gap the ML literature surfaces, and one category error to avoid:**

- *Real gap:* Dauphin, Pascanu, Gulcehre, Cho, Ganguli & Bengio (2014) show that in high-dimensional non-convex landscapes, saddle-point plateaus vastly outnumber true local minima, and are easy to mistake for convergence from the outside — the gradient (or here, the running-best score) looks flat whether the search has actually converged or is merely traversing a flat region a sufficiently different next attempt could still escape. `solver.py`'s plateau rule has no way to distinguish these two cases — the same ambiguity every fixed-window early-stopping rule in ML has. ML's actual mainstream answer is not a smarter epsilon but **warm restarts** (Loshchilov & Hutter 2017, SGDR): treat a detected plateau as a cue to *perturb and continue* rather than simply halt. Nothing in `solver.py`'s docstring currently tells the calling agent to do this — a genuine, citable documentation gap (§7, item 6).
- *Category error to avoid:* momentum and Adam (Sutskever, Martens, Dahl & Hinton 2013; Kingma & Ba 2015) accelerate an optimizer's *own* steps through flat regions using gradient history. `solver.py` does not propose candidates itself — the calling LLM does (`solver.py` docstring, Design Question 1) — so there is no "step" inside `solver.py` for momentum to accelerate. "Add momentum to `solver.py`" would be a category error; if this idea has any legitimate residue, it belongs to the *calling agent's* batch-construction strategy, outside `solver.py`'s own module boundary.

### 5b. Is `CORRELATION` genuinely like backpropagation's error feedback?

**Only partially — and the analogy breaks down exactly where the prompt asked it to be checked, so say so plainly.**

Where it is genuinely apt: both mechanisms exist so that "how wrong were we" is answered by arithmetic against real evidence, not by assumption or a plausible-sounding guess (Rumelhart, Hinton & Williams 1986). This is exactly why `CORRELATION`'s result stays `CALCULATED` provenance rather than becoming an LLM-estimated confidence score — the same instinct ADR-0014 already enforces.

**Where it breaks, and must not be pushed further:** backpropagation is continuous, proportional, and fully automatic — every training step, the chain rule computes exactly how much *each individual weight* in every earlier layer contributed to the output error, and applies a small, proportional correction to all of them, thousands to millions of times, with zero human judgment anywhere in the loop. `REDESIGN_DECISION`'s `"iterate"` is the opposite in every one of those respects: a single, discrete, **gated**, human decision (`REDESIGN_ACTIONS = {iterate, accept_design}`, `design_loop.py:171`), with no mechanism anywhere in the codebase for attributing the `CORRELATION` delta to any specific upstream assumption (there is no computed "how much should the NEC2 mesh-density assumption change because of this 2 dB gain discrepancy" the way there is a computed partial derivative for each weight). Pushing the analogy past "quantify, don't eyeball" to argue that `CORRELATION` should auto-propagate a correction — automatically retune a simulator bias, or automatically decide iterate-vs-accept — would argue for exactly the unattended correction loop ADR-0014 was written to forbid. **The analogy is genuinely informative at the "quantify the error, don't eyeball it" level; it is superficial pattern-matching past that point, and should not be used to justify automating `REDESIGN_DECISION`.**

### 5c. Does Neural Architecture Search support the human-gated `ARCHITECTURE` step, or argue for automating it?

**NAS is the single cleanest piece of supporting evidence for keeping `ARCHITECTURE` human-gated — not a case for loosening it — and this is fully developed in §2 above.** In summary: RL-based NAS (Zoph & Le 2017) needed ~800 GPUs for two weeks even on cheap CIFAR-10/PTB-scale evaluations; evolutionary NAS (Real, Aggarwal, Huang & Le 2019, AmoebaNet) needed 3,150 GPU-hours and *still* operated inside a human-designed cell template rather than inventing the scaffold itself; the cheaper differentiable/weight-sharing methods (DARTS — Liu, Simonyan & Yang 2019; ENAS — Pham, Guan, Zoph & Le 2018) only became cheap because the candidate operations were relaxed from a *small, closed, pre-enumerated* set — and the field's own reproducibility studies (Li & Talwalkar 2019; Yang, Esperança & Carlucci 2020) found that random search matches or beats these methods, and that the human-designed macro-structure contributes more to final performance than the automatically-searched micro-structure. Elsken, Metzen & Hutter's (2019) survey states plainly that architectures are still mostly hand-designed in the field that studies automating them.

**A future middle ground worth logging as fog, not as a recommendation:** DARTS/ENAS's actual trick — relax a small, closed, human-enumerated set of options into something continuously searchable — only becomes a real option *if* this repo ever wanted to search among a small, pre-enumerated set of known-good topology variants *within one already-approved design family* (e.g., choosing among three or four known feed-topology variants for a confirmed patch family). That is a materially narrower, different feature than today's open-ended `ARCHITECTURE` judgment, is not something the current evidence argues for building, and — echoing ADR-0014's own Consequences section, which leaves this door open only to a *future, separately-grilled* ADR — **pursuing it at all would require reopening ADR-0014 and issue #87's Out-of-Scope clause.** Logged in §7, item 9, as fog only.

## 7. The committed recommendation

**Name: Screen-Cheap, Confirm-Expensive, Satisfice-to-Accept.** Run the loop this way, end to end:

1. **Candidate-proposal strategy for `OPTIMIZATION`:** keep the LLM as the proposer (issue #87's own design — `solver.py` never proposes candidates itself), but make its proposals *evidence-guided* rather than ad hoc, using the machinery this repo already has: a two-tier funnel where a broad, LLM-proposed batch is screened cheaply at `ANALYSIS` (fast surrogate or closed-form calculation), narrowed to diversity-filtered top-scorers (Laumanns 2007's archive rule) before promotion to `SIMULATION`, and — once `SIMULATION` is genuinely expensive (map #104's full-wave Floquet solve) — searched within `OPTIMIZATION` using the *same* Gaussian-process-plus-Expected-Improvement recipe `optimization/bayesian.py` already implements (Jones, Schonlau & Welch 1998), repointed at that expensive objective instead of only the cheap closed-form one it searches today.
2. **Convergence/stopping rule:** keep `solver.py`'s existing three-way stop — `target_satisfaction_threshold`, `score_plateau`, `evaluation_budget` — exactly as coded. Do not replace `worst_of_scored_steps` or the plateau mechanism; Prechelt's (1998) patience-based early stopping is the field's own dominant default and this rule already matches it parameter-for-parameter. The one addition is **documentation, not code**: record, in `solver.py`'s docstring or `prompts/principal_engineer.md` (consistent with ADR-0010's own framing — steering lives in the prompt layer, not the module), that a `stop_reason='score_plateau'` report should be read by the calling agent as a cue to propose a *deliberately different* next batch (the SGDR/warm-restart move — Loshchilov & Hutter 2017) before concluding `OPTIMIZATION` is exhausted for this architecture, and only escalate to test-plan generation / `REDESIGN_DECISION` if *that* still plateaus.
3. **Macro completion:** adopt Simon's (1969) satisficing explicitly as the stated philosophy behind `REDESIGN_DECISION`'s `accept_design` branch — a design is done when its confirmed requirement targets clear their aspiration level at whatever evidence tier was actually achieved, with `CORRELATION`'s simulated-vs-measured delta as the signal deciding whether that tier is trustworthy enough to accept.
4. **Dependency handling:** keep the loop's shape exactly as it is — a single, strictly-ordered chain (Steward 1981) with exactly one feedback mark (`REDESIGN_DECISION → ARCHITECTURE`) and `solver.py`'s candidate evaluation **sequential**, not parallelized. Do not add a second, shorter tearing point to `REDESIGN_DECISION` itself modeled on `designs/lifecycle.py`'s `ANALYSIS` reentry — that reentry is a different mechanism, reached through a different tool, over a vocabulary that cannot express an architecture-level distinction in the first place (§2/§3). The real open question is narrower: whether `designs.status`'s rounding of every `"iterate"` up to `ANALYSIS` (ADR-0011) loses information a reader would want — collect the evidence Eppinger & Browning's (2012) method requires before deciding that.

**Justification, honestly, including where the literature disagreed:** Bandit theory (UCB1, Thompson sampling, Hyperband/BOHB) does not fit `solver.py`'s actual mechanics — it is a batch-scored-once module, not a revisit-and-reweight arm-puller — so it is deliberately not adopted here even though it was researched in full. Real-options theory and Set-Based Concurrent Engineering both genuinely argue for keeping multiple architectures alive longer than this codebase does — that tension is real, not manufactured, but ADR-0014 already weighed it against the project's single highest-priority rule (never build the first exception to "no autonomous manufacturing-adjacent commitment") and issue #87 sealed that decision at the spec level; reopening it is out of scope for this recommendation. The neural-network momentum/Adam analogy is a category error for `solver.py` specifically, because `solver.py` does not itself take optimization steps — that residue, if any, belongs to the calling LLM's own batch strategy, not to this module. And NAS — the ML analogue closest to the exact thing issue #87 forbids — turns out to *reinforce* the current design rather than undercut it: even ML's own most automation-friendly domain hasn't made full architecture automation the default, which is strong, non-strained evidence that this repo's stricter, human-gated posture is not behind the state of the art — it is, if anything, ahead of where NAS's own field has landed on trusting automation with architecture-level choices.
